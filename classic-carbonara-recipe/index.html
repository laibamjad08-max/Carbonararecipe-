Classic Carbonara Recipe

This classic carbonara recipe uses five ingredients and one pan: spaghetti, guanciale, egg, Pecorino Romano, and black pepper. There is no cream and no garlic. The sauce is made from egg yolks, cheese, and starchy pasta water, and it turns glossy when it hits the hot pasta.

If you have never made it, take your time with the egg step. Once you know why the sauce works, it takes about 25 minutes. For a simpler route with supermarket ingredients, try the easy carbonara recipe.

  • 10 minPrep
  • 15 minCook
  • 25 minTotal
  • 4Servings

Classic carbonara ingredients

This recipe serves four. Quality matters, because there are so few ingredients.

  • 400 g (14 oz) spaghetti
  • 150 g (5 oz) guanciale, cut into small strips or cubes (pancetta works too)
  • 4 large egg yolks plus 1 whole large egg
  • 60 g (about ¾ cup) finely grated Pecorino Romano, plus extra to serve
  • 1½ teaspoons freshly cracked black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon coarse salt for the pasta water (4 liters / 4 quarts of water)

Ingredient notes

Guanciale is cured pork cheek and gives the most traditional flavor. Pecorino Romano is a salty sheep's milk cheese. If you cannot find them, see the substitutions below.

How to make carbonara step by step

  1. Boil the water. Bring 4 liters of water to a boil in a large pot and add the salt. Guanciale and Pecorino are both salty, so do not add more.
  2. Crisp the guanciale. Place the pork in a cold, wide pan over medium-low heat. Cook 8 to 10 minutes until the fat has melted and the pieces are golden. Turn off the heat and leave the pork and fat in the pan.
  3. Make the egg mixture. In a bowl, whisk the yolks, whole egg, Pecorino, and black pepper into a thick paste.
  4. Cook the pasta. Cook the spaghetti 1 minute less than the package says, about 8 to 9 minutes, so it can finish in the pan. Scoop out 1 cup of pasta water, then drain.
  5. Combine off the heat. Add the pasta to the pan with the guanciale and toss. Let it cool for about 30 seconds, until it is hot but no longer sizzling.
  6. Add the sauce. Pour in the egg mixture with 3 tablespoons of pasta water. Toss quickly for 1 to 2 minutes. The residual heat thickens the sauce into a glossy coating.
  7. Adjust and serve. Add more pasta water a tablespoon at a time if it looks tight. Serve right away with extra Pecorino and pepper.

Tips for the best carbonara

  • Keep the pan off the heat when you add the eggs. Direct heat is the main cause of scrambling.
  • Grate the cheese finely. Coarse shreds can clump.
  • Use the pasta water. The starch helps the sauce cling. Our carbonara sauce guide explains the science in plain English.
  • Use room-temperature eggs so the mixture blends smoothly.
  • Serve immediately. The sauce tightens as it cools.

Common carbonara mistakes

  • Adding the egg to a pan that is still very hot, which scrambles it.
  • Draining the pasta and losing all the starchy water.
  • Adding cream, which hides the flavor and is not part of the traditional recipe.
  • Overcooking the pasta so it cannot absorb the sauce.
  • Using too much salt in the water.

If something goes wrong, read how to fix scrambled, dry, and watery carbonara.

Ingredient substitutions

  • No guanciale: use pancetta, or bacon for a smokier taste.
  • No Pecorino: use Parmesan, or half and half.
  • No spaghetti: rigatoni or bucatini also work.

Want to add protein? Try chicken carbonara. Prefer a richer, cream-style sauce? See the creamy carbonara recipe.

Storage and reheating

Carbonara is best eaten fresh. Cool leftovers quickly, refrigerate within two hours, and eat within one day. Reheat gently in a pan over low heat with a splash of water, stirring constantly. High heat can scramble the eggs. Freezing is not recommended because the sauce separates.

Frequently asked questions

Does authentic carbonara have cream?

No. The creaminess comes from egg yolks, cheese, and starchy pasta water. See our guide to carbonara recipe without cream for the full explanation.

Can I use bacon instead of guanciale?

Yes. Bacon is smokier and leaner, so the flavor changes, but it works well. Our easy carbonara recipe is built around bacon.

Can I use Parmesan instead of Pecorino Romano?

Yes. Parmesan is milder and nuttier. Many cooks use half Parmesan and half Pecorino for a softer flavor.

Does carbonara have raw egg?

The eggs are not boiled, but the heat from the pasta warms them until they thicken. They are not fully cooked in the way scrambled eggs are, so see our disclaimer about egg safety.

What pasta is best for carbonara?

Spaghetti is most common. Rigatoni, mezze maniche, and tonnarelli are also used in Rome.

Where did carbonara come from?

Carbonara is a Roman dish. Its exact origin is debated, and most accounts place its popularity in Italy in the middle of the 20th century.
